The attack

One RAW file is enough to plant your camera's fingerprint in another image. We did it. So the fingerprint alone is never the claim.

The claim

Only the camera's owner can register to it, and every registration is dated. A planted fingerprint can match a camera; it can't forge the owner's record.

Headline options

Switch in Tweaks. A: “Your camera already signs every photo. Genesis lets you prove it was yours.” B: “Every sensor leaves a birthmark. Genesis puts your name beside it.” C: “The photo will leave your hands. The record of where it came from stays.”

Hero + fingerprint motion

Hero stays light: no video, one 140px grain tile, a 30 fps pixel field where Bayer cells light up under the pointer with per-cell flicker like sensor noise (a slow drift on touch; static with reduced motion), and the Registered stamp settles once (overshoot to 1.0, ~500 ms). The fingerprint sequence is scroll-scrubbed across ~4.5 screens, sticky: zoom ×1→×7 into the photo; the RGGB mosaic fades in over it; sensor noise flickers then freezes into a fixed pattern; the frame pulls back and shrinks to a web copy with a JPEG block grid; a hairline draws to the record card and the stamp lands. Nothing loops; scrolling back reverses it exactly.

“The copy that already left”

Triggered once when a third of the section is visible. The web copy's metadata rows strike through one at a time (140 ms apart) and “stripped” appears; then the bracket joining both columns draws down (700 ms) and the shared Registered stamp settles. Without JavaScript, the final state shows.
